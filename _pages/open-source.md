I have started using open-source software and independent alternatives wherever practical. So far, the experiment is going well, although it remains difficult to avoid proprietary services, particularly on Android. Instead of Google Search, I use [DuckDuckGo](https://duckduckgo.com/); instead of WhatsApp, I use [Signal](https://signal.org/). I have also moved away from the Windows and macOS ecosystems to Linux, and now use [Arch Linux](https://archlinux.org/).

[Fedora](https://fedoraproject.org/), [openSUSE](https://www.opensuse.org/), and [Manjaro](https://manjaro.org/) are other alternatives to Windows and macOS, and there are many more distributions to explore. Open-source software and independent services are not the same thing: not every service mentioned here is open source.

There will always be some tension behind the slogan “only open source.” This website itself is hosted on GitHub, which is owned by Microsoft. I do not regard that as a reason to abandon the effort to support independent developers and open-source communities.

For me, the case for these alternatives goes beyond privacy. I am concerned about how much power we give a small number of technology companies by depending exclusively on their software and services. Supporting independent alternatives is one way of responding to that concern.
